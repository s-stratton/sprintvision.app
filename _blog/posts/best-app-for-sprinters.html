If you are a sprinter, the most useful thing an app can do is show you what you can’t feel. At full speed, each step lasts about a fifth of a second. You can’t tell from the inside whether your foot landed under your hips or out in front, whether you stood up after two steps or six, or whether your set position is actually the one your coach asked for. A camera can. A good app turns that video into answers.

What SprintVision does

SprintVision is a sprint analysis app for iPhone. You film a block start, acceleration or flying run, pick the clip from your camera roll and trim it to the run. Then it:

  • Tracks your whole body through every frame, even when the camera moves to follow you.
  • Marks every footprint on the track and measures each step’s length, ground contact, flight time and speed.
  • Rates your set position: front and rear knee and hip angles and your trunk, against the angles world-class sprinters hold.
  • Rates your acceleration: body lean, shin angle at touchdown and step rate over the first steps, against drive-phase targets.
  • Rates your top-speed mechanics: posture, shin and knee angle at touchdown, knee lift, heel recovery and arm action, against what sprinters at 9–11 m/s do.
  • Gives you a cue for anything outside the target range, so you know what to work on next session.
  • Shows your leg action changing from pushing to cycling as you build speed, and lets you freeze the run at key positions.
  • Gives you split times if you keep the start and finish in shot and mark them on the video.

Beyond the analysis

Good to know

  • iPhone. SprintVision is an iPhone app.
  • Film at normal speed. Real-time video keeps every step timed accurately. Ground contact and flight times are shown as estimates, because phone video is usually 30 frames per second (why that matters).
  • Film on the straight, with your whole body in shot. The filming guide covers the rest.

How to get started

  1. Download SprintVision from the App Store and start the free trial on a yearly plan.
  2. At your next session, film one rep: a block start, a 3-point start or a flying run.
  3. Pick the clip, trim it to the run, and see every step.
  4. Pick the one thing the analysis flags, train it, and film again in a week or two.
